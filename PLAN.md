# Echo: high-level plan

One sentence: Tutors need fresh practice problems that match the one a student just missed, and they can't trust AI-written ones unless every key is right. Echo turns a photo of that problem into up to three new ones. Every AI-written problem is checked by a math program first; where no checker exists yet, Echo shows verified problems from a prepared bank and labels them as not Echo-checked.

The moment that wins: In the chat, the checker rejects a wrong answer key, and the bot explains why using the checker's own numbers.

## What gets built (all of Calculus I and II)

- Upload a photo of a handwritten problem into a chat.
- The tutor confirms or edits the reading, and the topic Echo picked for it (one of the bank's topics).
- The tutor asks for three problems at a target level, 1 to 4.
- For a topic with an enabled verifier, the AI drafts five, SymPy checks each answer, and the first three that pass are shown with a checker log. For any other topic, up to three servable bank problems are shown, clearly labeled as not Echo-checked, or an unsupported notice if none are servable.
- The tutor can ask why one was rejected, ask for harder or easier, ask for a hint, or ask for a worksheet to download.

## Scope: all of Calculus I and II

- Echo covers every bank topic that has a servable pool. The bank has 90 topics in 10 units; the audit below can empty a topic (today two: `relative_extrema/variants/graph` and `increasing_decreasing/variants/graph`, whose 20 problems each all need a figure that is not in the repository), and a topic with no eligible problems, or none that parse into typed fields, is reported as unsupported: Echo tells the tutor so, and the topic is neither enabled nor counted in the coverage denominators until its figures are supplied or its entries repaired. Calculus I: limits and continuity, derivatives, applications of derivatives, integration basics, area and volume. Calculus II: integration techniques, applications of integration, parametric and polar, sequences and series, power series and Taylor. The bank has 3,510 problems: about 850 to 900 at each of levels 1 to 4 and 60 at level 5. Level 5 stays out of scope, which leaves 3,450 problems at levels 1 to 4. Answer formats in the bank are numeric (2,113), expression (706), enum (280), set (165), interval (150), and multi-part (96). An enum answer is a named outcome such as diverges, converges, infinite, does not exist, or absolutely or conditionally convergent; it is a category, not a multiple-choice list.
- Photo to topic: the photo reader turns the handwriting into typed math, and a topic classifier picks one of the bank's topics from that text, using each topic's title and its bank statements as the reference. The confirm screen shows the topic by title and lets the tutor change it, so a misclassification is fixed by the tutor before anything is generated. The topic is a tutor-confirmed field next to the typed problem fields.
- Typed problem fields are per topic. Each topic has a schema (for example limits: expression, point, direction; derivatives: expression, order, point; integrals: the two integral schemas below), and only typed fields, never raw text, reach SymPy. The confirmed problem also carries a tutor-confirmed answer type (numeric, expression, set, interval, enum, or multi-part). For a topic with an enabled verifier the verifier solves the confirmed problem and proposes the type. For a disabled topic, where Echo cannot solve it, the confirm screen defaults the type from the classifier and asks the tutor to confirm or change it, choosing from the types the bank actually has for that topic. For an enum or a limit, the tutor also sees which outcomes are possible (for example a number, infinity, or does not exist). The matching rule and the bank fallback use this confirmed type.

## Verification architecture: one verifier per topic

- Every topic needs a verifier before the AI may draft problems for it. A verifier takes the topic's typed fields and the drafted key, recomputes the answer independently from the problem alone with SymPy (never trusting the AI's key or steps), compares the two by answer type, and returns pass or fail with a log of the numbers it used. The log is what the bot quotes when it explains a rejection. What the tutor sees is what was verified: every displayed candidate's statement is rendered from its verified typed fields (task, conditions, and bounds included) by a fixed per-topic template. AI-written wording is shown only if re-parsing it yields exactly the same typed fields; otherwise the rendered template is shown instead, so an acceleration key can never sit beside a speed question.
- Verifiers share families, so 90 topics do not mean 90 separate engines. Antiderivative and definite-integral topics with an explicit integrand in x use the integral route below. Integral-property topics, where only the values of other integrals are given (for example `definite_properties`, such as finding the integral of f minus g from the integrals of f and g), use a separate linearity-and-orientation verifier: its typed fields are the supplied integral values, the bounds, and the combination asked for, and it recomputes the answer from linearity, bound reversal, and additivity over adjacent intervals. These topics are excluded from the antiderivative route. Derivative topics recompute with differentiation, limit topics with SymPy's limit, series topics with exact series tools, parametric and polar topics from the parametrization, and word-problem topics (related rates, optimization, motion) from a typed model of the situation. This family list is a starting point and may change as topics are built.
- Comparing keys by answer type: numeric answers by exact symbolic equality; expression answers by the difference simplifying to zero plus the whole-domain validity rule below; set and interval answers as whole sets, normalized (sorted, merged, duplicate-free) and then compared exactly with SymPy set equality, never by endpoints alone: finite sets must have the same members in both directions, so an extra or missing member such as `0` in `{-3, 1}` fails, and a union of intervals is compared component by component, including each endpoint and whether it is open or closed; enum answers by exact match between the recomputed category and the key's category label; multi-part answers require every part to pass its own comparison. When a topic can only be recomputed numerically (for example iterations or numerical approximations), a pass is labeled "numerically verified," never "exact," and that label travels with the problem.
- Gate: a topic's verifier is enabled only if it passes two bank tests. (1) Positive: the verifier accepts the bank's own key for every supported problem in the topic. Support is decided from the problem alone (see the support predicate below), never from the key, so the same rule applies to bank entries and to a freshly photographed problem. Problems that parse but are not supported (for example the improper integral `C410-06` or the non-substitution entry `C410-16`) are not tested and are not failures; they are reported separately and stay in the bank fallback. The parse rate is measured over the eligible set and must be at least 90%, and the problems a parser rejects are listed so the grammar can be extended rather than the threshold lowered. A topic is enabled only if (1) and (2) both pass on every supported problem, the parse rate is at least 90%, and at least 8 problems were gate-tested across at least min(3, L) distinct levels, where L is the number of levels 1 to 4 present in the topic's eligible pool, so a topic whose problems sit in only one or two levels (such as `definite_properties`, at levels 1 and 2) can still be enabled, while an empty or tiny topic never can. Any disagreement between the verifier and a bank key is treated as a bug in one of them: the topic stays disabled until it is resolved, the disagreement is recorded in `docs/known-issues.md`, and the disputed bank entry is quarantined, meaning removed from bank fallback, few-shot examples, the level reference, and every other use, and reported by the gate script, until its key is adjudicated. (2) Negative: for every bank problem used, a deliberately corrupted key (a changed constant, a flipped sign, a swapped operation, an unrelated expression of the same answer type, or for an enum a different valid outcome from the task's declared outcome vocabulary, even when the bank holds no example of it) is rejected, with no exceptions among corruptions that change the value, so a verifier that accepts everything cannot pass. For set, interval, and multi-part keys the corruptions must include adding a member, removing a member, changing an endpoint, flipping open or closed, dropping or adding a component of a union, and corrupting each part of a multi-part key on its own, and every one of these is rejected, so a verifier that accepts supersets or ignores openness cannot pass.
- Support predicate (problem-only): a problem is supported when its typed fields parse and the verifier can solve it from the problem alone, using no key. For the integral route this means deriving an antiderivative F with SymPy under the timeout (and, for a definite integral, also deriving a valid antiderivative F under the same restricted grammar and resource limits the checker uses, and confirming the integrand and F are defined and real on the closed interval between the bounds; a proper integrand whose antiderivative falls outside the grammar, such as `exp(-x^2)`, whose antiderivative needs `erf`, is unsupported), and for integration by substitution deriving an (f, g) pair from the integrand alone with the same derivation the bank adapter uses. If any step cannot be completed the problem is unsupported. The gate applies this predicate to bank entries and uses the key only afterward, for the comparison; wherever the substitution adapter below mentions using the bank's key to decide whether an entry passes the route, this predicate governs instead. The enabled-topic path applies the same predicate to the tutor's confirmed problem before drafting.
- Coverage is measured, not assumed. A gate script prints, per topic, ENABLED or DISABLED with the reason and the parse rate, and four separate counts: audited-eligible, parsed, verifier-supported, and gate-tested. The coverage total is enabled topics out of the topics that have eligible problems, and the number of gate-tested problems in enabled topics out of the eligible total, with every exclusion count printed next to it. A problem the gate never tested is never counted as covered. That printed number is what the pitch and the README quote.
- Build order: topics are built by bank problems covered per unit of effort, starting with the integral route (already specified below), then derivative rules, limits, and the rest. Each verifier is a separate worker brief whose "Done when" is the gate script printing ENABLED for that topic.

## What the tutor sees, by topic status

- Topic enabled, and the confirmed problem is inside the verifier's supported subset (the same support test the gate uses): the AI drafts five, the verifier checks each, and the first three that pass are shown, labeled Echo-checked (or numerically verified where that applies).
- Topic enabled, but the confirmed problem is outside the supported subset (for example an improper integral, whose endpoint singularity the integral route cannot check): the AI does not draft. The tutor sees a plain message that this kind of problem is not covered by the checker yet, and gets servable bank problems that match the confirmed topic, task, level, and answer type, labeled not Echo-checked, or an unsupported notice if none are servable.
- Topic not yet enabled: the AI does not draft. The tutor gets up to three servable problems from the bank at the requested level, topic, task, and confirmed answer type (or an unsupported notice if there are none), labeled "bank-verified, not Echo-checked." The tool stays useful on every topic that has a servable pool (defined under the bank audit below) and never claims more than was checked. As verifiers land, topics move from this fallback to Echo-checked. Pool sizes are measured after the audit, the answer-type match, and the distinctness filter, so a pool can have fewer than three: before any other filtering, 16 of the 477 topic, level, and answer-type pools at levels 1 to 4 already have fewer than three problems. When fewer than three are available the app shows what exists and says how many and why, never padding with problems of another type or level, and the gate script lists the short pools.

## Level check (all topics)

A draft's level is estimated against the bank. Compute simple size features of the problem and key (expression node count, nesting depth, number of distinct functions, key size), find the 5 nearest bank problems in the same topic by those features, and take the majority level. If no level has a majority (a tie or a three-way split), the draft is rejected, and the normal top-up and fallback rules apply. The draft passes only if that estimate equals the requested level. This replaces any per-topic rubric, needs no training, and is honest that levels are estimated from one tutor's ratings and not independently rated. A topic with fewer than 5 bank problems at the requested level skips drafting and uses the bank fallback if servable problems exist, and otherwise shows the unsupported notice.

Match to the confirmed problem (all topics): every candidate (AI draft, bank top-up, or template) must have the same topic as the confirmed problem, the same task, the same answer type, and the requested level, and must differ from the confirmed problem and from the other accepted candidates, deduplicated by normalized typed fields. A topic may add a structure rule on top; integration by substitution does, below. A structure rule applies to every candidate that is shown, including bank fallback in a topic whose verifier is disabled, so a bank entry that fails the rule (such as `C410-16` for a substitution request) is not servable for that topic. Task: some topics mix different skills (in `motion`, `MV01` asks for acceleration and `MV02` asks for speed, both level-1 numeric problems), so each topic's schema declares a `task` field listing what a problem can ask for, or is flagged single-task after a quick review of its bank entries. The adapter derives each bank problem's task with a per-topic rule applied to its statement, the confirm screen shows the task for the tutor to confirm or change, and AI drafts and bank fallback must match it. A topic with no declared task list can still serve bank fallback, but those problems are labeled "same topic; the specific skill may differ," and the topic cannot be enabled until its tasks are declared. The gate script lists topics whose tasks are undeclared. Each task whose answer is an enum also declares its allowed outcome vocabulary (for example, series convergence: converges, diverges, absolutely convergent, conditionally convergent; limits: a number, infinity, negative infinity, or does not exist). In the bank, `geometric`, `limits`, `monotone_bounded`, `recursive`, `telescoping`, and `u_substitution` each show only one enum outcome at levels 1 to 4 ("diverges"), so the vocabulary, not the bank, supplies the wrong outcome for the negative test.

## Integral route (what "computer-checked" means for answers that are integrals)

This route verifies any topic whose answer is an antiderivative or the value of a definite integral with an explicit integrand in x (substitution, basic antiderivatives, trig integrals, by parts, partial fractions, trig substitution, and similar). The rules in this section are the hardened contract for that route. Other topics use their own verifier under the architecture above, with the same input boundary, resource limits, tolerance rule, and whole-domain validity where they apply.

- Two problem types, two checker routes. Indefinite: an indefinite integral of one expression in `x` with an antiderivative key (constants of integration ignored). Definite: an integrand in `x`, a lower bound, an upper bound, and an exact-value key. Each type has its own typed schema: indefinite is {integrand, variable, domain}; definite is {integrand, variable, lower, upper}. The tutor-confirm screen and the photo mapper produce one of these two schemas, and everything below that says "domain" applies to the indefinite type only.
- Input boundary: problems, keys, and tutor-edited readings are parsed with a restricted grammar (numbers, `x`, `+ - * / ^`, parentheses, and a whitelist of functions: sin, cos, tan, sec, csc, cot, asin, acos, atan, sinh, cosh, tanh, abs, exp, log, sqrt). Raw text is never passed to `eval` or to SymPy's eval-based parsing. Expressions are capped at 200 characters, and each check has a 5-second timeout. Parse depth is capped at 20 and any exponent must be an integer or rational with absolute value at most 20, so `9^(9^9)` is rejected at parse time. Symbolic work runs in a separate worker process with the 5-second wall-clock limit and a memory limit of 512 MB, and the worker is killed on either limit, which counts as a rejected candidate. Substitution candidates carry two extra typed fields, `g` (an expression in `x`) and `f` (an expression in `u`), added to both candidate schemas. Each is parsed with the same restricted grammar configured for its one permitted variable, `u` for `f` and `x` for `g`, before any symbolic verification, and the same length, depth, and exponent caps apply.
- Structured input: a problem is one of the two typed schemas above (indefinite: integrand, variable, domain; definite: integrand, variable, lower, upper). The tutor-confirm screen shows the problem as an integral with `dx` but edits the fields of whichever type was read, and the tutor can switch type. Only the extracted `integrand`, `key`, and definite bounds and keys pass through the restricted grammar. Integral notation and domain text are never parsed by it. The grammar whitelists the constants `pi` and `e` and safely parses them as symbols, never via eval, so bounds and keys such as `pi/4` and `log(2)` are valid. The photo reader's output is mapped into these fields before the tutor confirms.
- Domain representation: the domain is a typed single open interval (a, b), where each endpoint is a rational, a multiple of pi, or infinity, chosen from a menu or parsed by a restricted interval grammar. Empty, reversed, or unparseable domains are rejected. The displayed domain is rendered from this typed value, and the checker samples and validates exactly that value, so what is shown is what was checked.
- Whole-domain validity (one rule for everything): for the integrand, the key, and the key's derivative, two checks must both pass. (1) Source check: the restricted-grammar parse tree of the drafted expression, before any SymPy simplification or cancellation, is walked and every denominator must be nonzero, every log argument positive, every sqrt argument nonnegative, every tan, sec, csc, and cot argument away from its poles, and every asin and acos argument within [-1, 1] on the entire stated interval, so a hole like `(x-1)/(x-1)` in a drafted key is caught even though SymPy would cancel it. (2) Simplified check: SymPy's continuous-domain analysis must show the simplified expression is defined, real, and continuous on the entire interval. The key is displayed as drafted, never silently replaced by its simplified form. Anything either check cannot establish is rejected. Real-valuedness is proven separately, because continuous-domain analysis alone does not guarantee real values: the variable is declared real, every power with a non-integer exponent (including fractional powers and constants like `(-1)^(1/2)`) is treated like a sqrt and its base must be nonnegative on the interval, and the whole expression must be shown real by SymPy's real-valued test and also have zero imaginary part at every sample point. Anything not provably real is rejected. A problem whose integrand fails this is rejected or has its interval narrowed to a sub-interval where it holds, and the narrowed interval is what is displayed. This replaces per-function special cases: a zero of a sqrt argument is not a failure by itself, so `sqrt(x^4)` is a valid key for `2x` on all reals, while `log(-x^2)` on x > 0 and `tan(x)^2/2` across pi/2 are rejected. In the source check, every negative power counts as a denominator: its base must be proven nonzero on the whole interval, so a key like `x^-1*x` is rejected on (-inf, inf) even though it simplifies to 1.
- Pass predicate: the derivative of the key minus the integrand simplifies to 0, and a numeric spot check at 5 sample points inside the stated interval agrees under the numeric tolerance rule below, in addition to whole-domain validity. When the tutor gives no domain, the default is the first interval from this fixed list on which the integrand passes whole-domain validity: (-inf, inf), (0, inf), (-inf, 0), (1, inf), (-inf, -1), (-1, 1), (0, 1). If none passes, the tutor must enter a domain. Endpoints stay rational, multiples of pi, or infinity, so algebraic endpoints such as sqrt(2) are not supported and a tutor needing one chooses a representable sub-interval.
- Numeric tolerance rule: two values a and b agree when |a - b| <= 1e-9 * max(|a|, |b|), with a tiny absolute floor of 1e-12 only so values near zero are not compared against nothing. This is a relative scale, so large and small answers are held to the same standard. It applies only to spot checks and cross-checks; it never proves a key exact, which is always symbolic.
- Structure rule for integration by substitution (separate from answer correctness, and one example of a per-topic structure rule). Level is checked by the all-topic level check above. Substitution rule: the candidate must come with its intended pair (f, g), and the checker verifies that f(g(x))*g'(x), times a constant, equals the integrand symbolically, where f is a nonconstant function of u alone (it contains no x) and g is not the bare variable. In addition, a structural test runs on the drafted, unexpanded integrand's parse tree: it must contain a composition node, meaning a function call or power whose argument or base is an inner expression other than `x` or a constant multiple of `x` alone. So `(5x+3)^3` and `(3x-1)^5` pass, because the base 5x+3 is a genuine inner expression, while `x^2` and `3x^4` fail the structural test and are treated as power-rule problems even though a degenerate pair such as g = x^3 or g = x^2 could be written for them. The inner expression is normalized (expanded and simplified) before the identity and constant-multiple exclusions are applied, and the composition must remain nontrivial after normalization, so `(x+1-1)^2` normalizes to `x^2` and is treated as a power-rule problem. The structural test runs on the whole integrand after normalization, not just the drafted tree: the qualifying composition node must survive normalization of the whole integrand (so `x^2 + sin(x+1) - sin(x+1)`, which normalizes to `x^2`, has no composition and fails), and its inner expression must equal the verified pair's g up to normalization, so the composition that passes the test is the one the (f, g) pair actually uses.
- Integration by substitution, match to the confirmed problem (bank entries use the (f, g) pair the adapter derived for them, so `C410-11` with an outer power cannot top up a request built on `C410-05` with an outer sine): on top of the all-topic matching rule, every candidate (AI draft, bank top-up, or template) must share the confirmed problem's inner-function family (for example polynomial inside cos, versus exp or log) and its outer-function family. Candidates that match the level but not the family are rejected the same way. The family is read from the confirmed `integrand` using the same restricted grammar. Candidates (AI drafts, bank top-ups, and templates) must also match the confirmed problem's type, definite or indefinite, unless the tutor explicitly asks to change it, so a confirmed definite integral is never answered with indefinite ones.

## Problem bank (docs/verified_problems.json)

- Source: `docs/verified_problems.json` is a pre-built bank of 3,510 verified Calculus I and II problems in 90 topics, each with a level, a statement, a LaTeX answer, steps, and, for 3,320 of the 3,510 problems, a SymPy answer string. The other 190 are enum answers, whose canonical key is the normalized category text in the LaTeX answer (for example `\text{diverges}` becomes `diverges`). Levels 1 to 4 are used; level 5 (60 problems) stays out of scope. Integration by substitution is its `u_substitution` topic: 50 problems, 10 at each level 1 to 5; of these 27 are indefinite integrals, 21 are definite integrals with exact numeric answers, and 2 are "diverges" cases.
- Roles: the bank is the ground truth for the verifier gate (positive and negative tests), the few-shot examples for the AI drafts, the reference for the level check and for topic classification, the fallback problems for topics with no enabled verifier, and the top-up when fewer than three drafts pass (before generic templates). It is not served as the answer to a photo unless the confirmed problem matches it.
- Bank audit (all topics): before any bank problem is used for anything, including fallback to a tutor, an audit script checks that its statement is self-contained: the supplied facts determine the requested answer: every function or symbol the answer depends on is either defined in the statement or constrained by stated values that suffice (unspecified `f` and `g` are allowed when their stated integral values determine the answer, as in `C44-01`), and nothing refers to a figure, graph, table, or diagram that is not given as text or an asset in the repository. Problems that fail are excluded everywhere and listed in a report. Examples found so far: `OS19`, `OS22`, `OS29`, `OS35`, and `OS39` ask for a limit of an undefined `f(x)`, and `RG01` to `RG20` and `IG01` to `IG20` refer to figures that do not exist in the repository. Excluded problems are never served as bank-verified problems; they may later be repaired and re-admitted.
- Eligible set: the problems at levels 1 to 4 that pass the audit. Every coverage figure and every pool size below is measured over this eligible set, and the number excluded is reported separately.
- Servable pool: the part of the eligible set whose problems also parse into typed fields and are not quarantined, so their task, answer type, and equivalence to a confirmed problem can be checked. Only servable problems are shown as bank fallback. A topic whose servable pool is empty at the confirmed level, task, and answer type shows the unsupported notice. The gate script and every product claim distinguish audit-eligible topics from servable topics, and the coverage line reports both counts.
- Bank adapter (all topics): each topic has a parser that reads the bank statement and answer into that topic's typed fields. The gate script reports the parse rate per topic. Bank problems a parser cannot read are excluded from the gate, few-shot examples, the level reference, and same-task bank fallback, because without typed fields their task and their equivalence to the confirmed problem cannot be checked. They stay out until their entries parse.
- Definite route: bounds and the key are constant expressions, parsed by the same restricted grammar applied to numbers only (whitelisted functions allowed, so `log(2)`, `pi/4`, and `sqrt(2)` are valid; the domain-endpoint restriction to rationals and multiples of pi applies only to indefinite domains). The key is exact. A definite candidate must come with an antiderivative F. F must pass the full indefinite check on the closed interval between the bounds (integrand and F both defined, real, and continuous there, using the same source-tree and simplified checks), and the key must equal F(upper) - F(lower) symbolically, proven exact by SymPy; if exact equality cannot be established the candidate is rejected. Numeric quadrature (mpmath, under the numeric tolerance rule below) is used only as an extra cross-check and a labeled sanity test, never as the proof of an exact key. Improper integrals (an integrand undefined at a bound, such as bank entry C410-06) and "diverges" problems are never AI-generated or checked by this route. Before the interval is built, each bound must be proven finite and real (for example `sqrt(-1)` and `1/0` are rejected) and their order must be established so the interval between them can be formed; either order is accepted, and the original order is kept in F(upper) - F(lower), so the integral from 1 to 0 of x dx has key -1/2. If finiteness, realness, or order cannot be established the candidate is rejected. Equal bounds are valid: the integrand must be defined at the common bound and the key must be exactly 0, and the interior spot check is skipped because there is no interior.
- Bank adapter for integration by substitution: before any bank entry is used for top-ups, few-shot examples, or tests, a bank adapter reads it and keeps only entries whose statement, bounds, and answer parse into one of the two typed schemas and pass the route above (using the bank's own antiderivative-style `answer.sympy` for indefinite entries, and for definite entries re-deriving F with SymPy and filtering out any it cannot). Improper, divergent, and unparseable entries are filtered out of the top-up and few-shot pools. Divergent and improper entries may still be served to a tutor as bank problems clearly labeled as bank-verified rather than Echo-checked, or omitted; the adapter reports how many entries it kept per level, and the substitution part of the gate's test set is drawn only from kept entries. Conversion is deterministic: a LaTeX-to-fields extractor reads `\int`, the optional bounds, the integrand, and `dx` from the statement, and a syntax converter maps bank expression strings to the restricted grammar (`**` becomes `^`, `exp(...)` and `sqrt(...)` stay, `pi` and `e` are the whitelisted constants). Entries the extractor or converter cannot handle are filtered. The retained count per level is verified and printed before the gate's substitution test set are selected. The adapter also applies the substitution rule (the (f, g) pair check and the structural composition test) to every entry before it can enter the few-shot pool or the gate's substitution test set, so entries that carry a correct key but are not substitution problems (for example C410-16, whose only composition is `exp(-4x)`) are excluded. The adapter reports the eligible count per level, after both the route check and the substitution rule, before the gate's substitution test set are selected. Bank entries have no f or g, so the adapter derives a pair for each one: it collects the distinct inner expressions of the normalized integrand's composition nodes as candidate g values and, for each, forms f(u) by rewriting integrand / g'(x) in terms of u = g(x), by solving u = g(x) for x where g is invertible and otherwise by direct substitution of g by u. A pair is accepted only if the resulting f contains no x and the pair passes the same verification as a drafted pair (f(g(x))*g'(x), times a constant, equals the integrand symbolically, plus the structural test). The first accepted pair is stored with the entry; an entry with no accepted pair is filtered out of the few-shot and test pools. The adapter reports how many entries were kept per level after this step.
- Honest labeling: the bank, the plan, and the code were prepared before the event. The organizer approved writing code beforehand, provided the final submission is made before the deadline. Bank levels were rated by one tutor and have not been checked by a second rater.

## Generation guarantee

The AI drafts five candidates. Candidates that pass both checks are shown, up to three. This applies to topics with an enabled verifier. If fewer than three pass, the app tops up first from servable bank problems at the same topic and level (labeled bank-verified), then from offline templates where the topic has them (labeled template-generated; every template problem and key first passes the same topic verifier, level check, and matching checks as an AI draft, and a template that fails is rejected). Templates exist only for integration by substitution; every other topic's offline fallback is the bank. Topics without an enabled verifier skip drafting and show servable bank problems or the unsupported notice, as described above. If three still can't be produced, the app shows what it has and says so. Before any candidate is shown it must differ from the confirmed problem and from the other accepted candidates: candidates equivalent to the confirmed problem are rejected, and accepted candidates are deduplicated by their normalized per-topic typed fields (for integral problems: integrand, problem type, and definite bounds), so five copies of one problem count as one in any topic.

## Biggest risk and fallback

The photo read or the AI calls fail on venue wifi. The tutor confirm screen fixes misreads, a labeled saved replay covers slow calls, the offline template generator covers an AI outage, and a backup video covers a dead laptop. The checker itself runs locally. A second risk is that fewer than 90 verifiers land by Friday. The gate and the bank fallback make that safe: a topic without a passing verifier shows servable bank problems labeled bank-verified, not Echo-checked, or an unsupported notice when none exist, and the coverage number is reported as measured.
